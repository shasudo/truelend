When money is needed fast, most people reach for whichever credit is nearest — usually the card
in their wallet. Speed feels free. It isn't. The three common ways to borrow ₹2 lakh for a year
have wildly different price tags.

## The honest comparison

**Revolving on a credit card** — paying the minimum due and carrying the balance — costs
3.0–3.5% _per month_. Annualised with fees, that's **40%+ APR**, the most expensive formal
credit in India. Revolving should be treated as an emergency measure, never a plan.

**Card EMI conversion** — most issuers will convert a large purchase into 6–24 EMIs at
13–18% p.a. plus a processing fee. Reasonable for a one-off purchase, but each conversion locks
a slice of your credit limit and keeps utilisation high, which quietly drags your CIBIL score.

**A personal loan** — 10.5–16% p.a. for decent profiles, fixed EMIs, a defined end date, and no
impact on card utilisation. For anything above ₹1 lakh needed for more than three months, it's
usually the cheapest and the most disciplined option.

On that ₹2 lakh for twelve months: roughly **₹13,000 of interest on a personal loan at 12%**,
versus **₹40,000+ revolving on a card**. Same money, three times the price.

## The decision in four questions

1. **How long will I need it?** Under 45 days — a card's interest-free window is genuinely
   free; use it and pay in full. Longer — term credit wins.
2. **Is the amount fixed or open-ended?** A fixed need suits a loan; a fluctuating one might
   justify a card or an overdraft, used carefully.
3. **What's my utilisation doing?** If the spend pushes any card above ~30% of its limit for
   months, your score pays a tax on top of the interest.
4. **Can I prepay?** Personal loans allow foreclosure after 6–12 EMIs (watch the 2–4% charge);
   card EMI conversions often charge more to exit early.

## The consolidation case

If you're already revolving on one or more cards, a personal loan taken _to clear them_ is one
of the few borrowing moves that's almost always right: interest drops from ~40% to ~12–14%,
utilisation falls, and your score usually rises within two cycles — which then prices your
next, bigger loan better. The one rule: the cards must stay paid-in-full afterwards, or the
hole just gets deeper.

## Match the instrument to the need

Cards are a payments tool with a 45-day float. Personal loans are a financing tool with a
defined exit. Trouble starts when either is used as the other. Before you swipe, convert or
sign — know which tool the moment actually calls for.
